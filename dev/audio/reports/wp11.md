# WP11 report: Mac latency of PC sound/mic, drift integral at its clamp (`audio/wp11`)

**Done.** Mac backend: IOProcs ask for 128-frame IO buffers (was 480; `BEAMER_AUDIO_MAC_BUFFER_FRAMES` still overrides). The size is per process: while the helper rendered at 128, another process read 512 and got 512-frame callbacks (94/s, measured twice, also during a live 10 min run); the helper puts back the value it found when its last Port on a device closes (`backend_check play` reports before/while/after: 512/128/512). Skipped IO cycles are counted and logged (0 in every live run at 128). Engine: the jitter buffer measures the sender/render clock ratio from timestamps and the drift integral follows it (below); its target keeps a 10 ms render-period term (`period_floor_ms`), because at 128 frames the target fell 20 -> 13 ms and PC sound underran more (3.4-3.8 vs 2.7 expansions/min, 3-7x the detector glitches in runs b_after2/3, b_gap1). Stats add `ppm_est`, `ppm_est_raw`, `ppm_est_se`. Beamer runs 62beabc on the Mac, route idle (mic off, outputs local); the PC was not redeployed (6487938).

**Root cause of the drift integral at +200 ppm.** Not a clock. Measured: Mac speakers vs host clock +1.9..+2.3 ppm (silent IOProc, 0 gaps at 480/128/512); PC clock vs Mac +17.3..+20.2 ppm (pings, every run); so the clocks differ by ~16-17 ppm (Phase 1: ~17). In the WP10 soak the receiver applied +400 ppm on average because of level the jitter buffer itself adds: each expansion whose late frame then arrives plays 10 ms of PLC without advancing the timeline (+10 ms of level). 112 expansions vs 51 drops/flushes in 31 min = +0.6 s of level = ~+330 ppm of "drift"; the PI integrated it and sat at its clamp. Reproduced in simulation (`jb_drift_integral_with_expansions`: +20 and -60 ppm clocks with late bursts -> integral +200 before, exactly +20/-60 after). Fix: per second the lowest transit (arrival - ts/48k) and the lowest pull lag (now - pulled/48k); the slope of their difference, least squares over up to 10 min with one offset per segment (a step of > 15 ms for 20 s starts a segment), cutting points below the line harder (extra delay only lowers it). Valid after 60 s with standard error <= 5 ppm; the integral then moves to it at <= 2 ppm/s and keeps its value while the estimate is not valid. Two earlier versions failed live and are fixed in later commits (a 20 s window read -977 ppm; a window restarted inside a Wi-Fi episode read -177): both cases are now engine tests. Also found: PC encoded frames run 4-174 ppm slower than the PC clock in many runs, i.e. the PC capture (process loopback) loses audio; each loss is a level step with no jitter-buffer event and a click on the Mac (b_f1: 20 ms vanished at 91 s, 15 glitches around it). This, not the clocks, is what count-based rates see; the timestamp estimate ignores such steps.
