Date: 2026-11-11. Branch `feat/camera`. Prototype, raw tables and how to reproduce:
`dev/camera/proto/transport/` (`README.md`, `out/*.summary.json`, `out/q3_*.md`, `out/q4_sweep.md`).

## Method

- **Probe, not engine** (`vprobe.py`, Python like the audio transport prototype). What is measured is the
  air path and the PC's radio, not CPU: rates are 300-550 packets/s, and the Python sender is on time (audio
  sends p50 0.4 ms / p99 1.2 ms late). AEAD is left out, but every packet has the exact sealed size of the
  proposed format. A C++ probe would measure the same Wi-Fi and take a day longer to build.
- **Mac `send`**: 30 fps access units. Sizes: P-frames lognormal (CV 0.34), keyframes every 2 s (to get
  300 keyframe samples per run; production sends almost none) at 80/120 KB (720p) or 160/256 KB (1080p).
  The average equals the target rate (2.5 / 5 Mbit/s, checked: 2.49-5.01). C1's encoder traces
  (`proto/mac/out`) confirm the order of size: 720p 2.5 Mbit/s P 10.1 KB, IDR 120 KB, LTR refresh
  12.3-14.2 KB; 1080p 5 Mbit/s P 20.3 KB, IDR 256 KB. Fragments of <= 1148 B, plus 52 B of headers and tag.
  Sending is either a burst, paced at 2.5x the bitrate, or a token bucket. An 80 B audio-like packet every
  10 ms goes on **the same socket** (one flow, as in the engine); pings 5/s.
- **PC `recv`** (`C:\Users\Alex\camera-c3`): logs each arrival (QPC) and answers pings. Like the engine, it
  sends a 46 B receiver report every 10 ms, which keeps the RZ616 radio awake (audio W3). Ports: PC UDP
  24850, Mac 40150. The PC punches first, so no firewall rule on either side.
- **Clock**: per 5 s, the lowest-RTT ping, then a linear fit (drift 19-25 ppm, residual 0.04-0.2 ms).
  "Network delay" of a frame = arrival of its last packet minus the send of its last packet. "Total" =
  arrival of its last packet minus the encoder output time, so it includes the pacing.
- **Runs**: 10 min per UDP configuration (8 runs, audio-only included), 5 min per TCP configuration
  (3 runs). The TCP runs go through `ssh -L` to a loopback listener on the PC (over Tailscale, direct on the same Wi-Fi), a stand-in
  for the Beamer link.
- **Recovery and buffer** (`sim.py`): the measured per-packet delay/loss of a run is replayed under each
  policy. Feedback takes the measured delay of the PC's next receiver report. Today's Wi-Fi lost almost
  nothing outside the keyframe-triggered holes and had no 1.5 s stall, so policies are also compared under
  **stress overlays added to the measured delays** (labelled): Gilbert bursts 0.5 % (mean burst 6
  packets), iid 1 %, a 1.5 s total loss every 5 min (`stall`), a 1.5 s hold-and-release every 5 min
  (`hold`, what the AP does when the PC dozes).

What the tables say:

- **The Wi-Fi carries 5 Mbit/s of video without trouble.** Outside the keyframe-triggered holes below, loss
  is rare and short (1-5 packets, 0-2 times per 10 min). The delay tail (p99 22-34 ms) is the same with and without video and the same at
  2.5 and 5 Mbit/s: it is Wi-Fi weather, not load. Spike timeline (`analyze.py` lists every episode): 0.2-6
  episodes > 45 ms per minute depending on the run. One episode of 3.6 s with packets 50-148 ms late and
  none lost (`v720b` t=185 s: the AP held them, the "hold" pattern). One 0.5 s stall during `t720` (UDP
  pings at the same moment: RTT 497 ms). **No 1.5 s stall in ~95 min of runs today**; the audio work saw
  0.66-1.46 s ones, so they are covered by the overlays below.
- **Video does not disturb audio on the shared flow.** Audio-like p95 is 6.8 ms alone and 5.9-8.6 ms with
  video; p99 is 23-33 ms in every run. Audio packets sent within 40 ms after a keyframe burst: p95 8.4 vs
  7.2 ms elsewhere (720p burst), 6.6 vs 7.0 ms (1080p burst). The flow does not need a second socket or a
  priority scheme for delay.
- **Except when a large keyframe goes out fast.** In `v1080b` (unpaced bursts), 3 of 300 keyframes lost their
  tail (fragments 104-184, from the ~126th packet on) and the next frame's head: 28, 57 and 82 packets in
  a row. **The 5-6 audio packets sent during those bursts were lost too** (50-60 ms of sound, more than
  Opus FEC repairs). It is not the Mac's queue (FQ-CoDel on en0: 0 drops; `sndq_maxlen` 128) and not the
  PC (UDP receive errors 0, NIC discards 0). It is the Wi-Fi driver or the AP during a moment of
  contention. 720p bursts (~70 packets) never lost a keyframe packet. Real 1080p IDRs from VideoToolbox
  are 256 KB (C1), i.e. ~225 packets.
- **A token bucket (32 packets of burst, then 25 Mbit/s) helps but does not settle it.** P-frames stay
  unpaced (<= 18 packets). `v1080h` (160 KB keyframes) and `v1080k` (C1's real 256 KB) lost 3-4
  packets each, none in a keyframe, no audio. But `v720h` (120 KB) had one 27-packet hole from fragment
  97 of a keyframe through the next two frames, and 6 audio packets with it. **Over the whole data set,
  the 4 holes longer than 5 packets all began inside a large keyframe sent at >= 25 Mbit/s**; the other
  losses are 1-5 packets at random times. Keyframes sent fast occupy 0.4-1.5 % of the airtime, so this
  is not chance.
- **Pacing every frame at 2.5x the bitrate** never lost a keyframe packet (`v720p`, `v1080p`: 600
  keyframes, 4 lost packets in total, none in a keyframe). But an 80-160 KB keyframe then takes ~107 ms
  to leave and arrives 111 ms after encoding (p50). The 2-3 frames behind it also wait (P-frame total p99
  99 ms against 32 ms in burst mode). That is acceptable for a frame that occurs once per stream start,
  not every 2 s.
- **Consequence for the design**: avoid IDRs (LTR refresh instead, Q3). Send P/LTR frames through the
  32-packet / 25 Mbit/s bucket (in effect unpaced), and pace the rare IDR at ~2.5-4x the bitrate:
  ~100 ms once, at stream start or as a last resort.
- **Receiver reports** (PC -> Mac, the feedback path for NACK/PLI): p50 2.3-3.0 ms, p99 23-33 ms.

## Q1: wire design (concrete, against `native/audio/engine`)

**Same flow, not a separate one.** The measurement above shows no delay cost for audio on the shared
socket. A second flow would double the punch/fallback machinery (`Flow` state machine, `audio_flow`
offer/answer, keys, RR) for no gain. Under contention both flows share the same radio anyway. Rule: one
flow per direct peer carries every stream, audio and video, both directions. This requires the camera
sender and receiver to live in the same media helper process as audio, i.e. the Mac capture/encode of C1
and the PC hand-off of C2 plug into the engine as stream kinds.

**Packet** (`packet.h`, version stays 1; old peers never get a video stream because it is negotiated):

- 24 B header as today: `ptype=kMedia`, new `codec=kCodecH264 (2)`, `stream`, `seq` (per stream, every
  packet including retransmissions of new data; also the NACK key), `ts` = **capture time** of the frame in
  90 kHz units of the sender's stream clock (stream start = 0; wraps after 13 h), `nsamp`=0,
  `channels`=0, `reserved`=0.
- AEAD payload = 12 B video header + fragment. Video header (big endian): `frame u32` (+1 per access
  unit), `frag u16`, `nfrag u16`, `vflags u8` (bit0 IDR, bit1 LTR refresh, bit2 this frame is an LTR,
  bit3 discardable), `ltr u8` (LTR token of this frame, or of the reference used by a refresh),
  `rsv u16`. Fragment: <= 1148 B of the Annex-B access unit, cut at any byte (the receiver concatenates
  by `frag`). Total UDP payload <= 1200 B, which leaves room for Tailscale (MTU 1280) and any VPN; the
  engine's `kMaxPayload` 1400 stays the parse limit.
- **Retransmission = the identical sealed bytes** (same seq, same nonce, same plaintext: no nonce reuse
  problem), kept in a 1 s history (625 KB at 5 Mbit/s). That needs a **replay window of 1024 for
  video streams** (`ReplayWindow` with 16 u64 words, templated on size). A 1080p IDR is ~225 packets and
  a NACK arrives after the burst, so 128 is too small.
- New ptypes, all sealed with the stream key in the receiver direction (`stream | kDirBit`), like `kRR`:
  - `kNack = 7`: up to 32 entries of `(seq_base u64, mask u16)`, sent at once when a hole is seen (next
    packet arrives with a gap: reorder was 0 in every run), repeated after max(2 x RTT, 15 ms), at most 3
    times, only while the frame's deadline has not passed and only for holes <= 64 packets (a bigger hole
    is a stall: go straight to `kPli`).
  - `kPli = 8`: `last_decoded_frame u32`, `acked_ltr u8`, `reason u8`. Repeated every 300 ms while the
    PC cannot decode. The sender answers with an **LTR refresh** referencing `acked_ltr` if it still has
    it, else an IDR.
  - `kSr = 9` (sender -> receiver, sender direction, stream key), every 1 s per stream:
    `(ts u32, sender_mono_ns u64)`. It maps each stream's `ts` onto the sender's clock, for audio (48 kHz
    sample count) and video (90 kHz capture time) alike: lip sync, see below.
- **Receiver report for a video stream** (`kRR`, payload 6 -> 24 B): `newest_ts u32`, `level u16`
  (playout margin in 0.1 ms = deadline minus completion, min over the interval), `hi_seq_lo u32`,
  `lost u32` (cumulative, after repair), `late u16` (frames not shown, cumulative), `acked_frame u32`
  (newest frame decoded; it acknowledges LTRs for `AcknowledgedLTRTokens`), `recv_kbps u16`, `flags u8`
  (bit0 decoding stalled), `rsv u8`. Today `Flow::send_rr` sends one RR per 10 ms round-robin across
  receiving streams, and only when this side sent nothing in 10 ms. Video needs its RR at least every
  50 ms even when audio flows the other way (`max(rr_ms, 50 ms)` per stream, round-robin unchanged).
- **Pacing**: a per-flow **token bucket** for video packets: capacity 32 packets, refill 25 Mbit/s
  for P and LTR-refresh frames (in effect unpaced: they are 9-20 packets); **IDR frames at 2.5-4x the
  bitrate** (`vflags` bit0 tells the pacer). Audio and control packets bypass it (sent at once by their
  threads, as today). Retransmissions go through the bucket ahead of new data. The jitter buffer's
  statistics exclude IDR frames, which arrive ~100 ms late by design; the display simply resumes on
  them. The pacer can be a small
  thread of the video send stream (`std::condition_variable` wait until the next token). The 2 ms
  `NetLoop` poll is too coarse at 25 Mbit/s (one 1200 B packet every 0.38 ms).
- **Control on the link** (`audio_*` family, PHASE2 §5): `audio_hello.features += "video1"`;
  `audio_open {kind:"video", codec:{"name":"h264","profile":"high","w":1280,"h":720,"fps":30,"bitrate":2500000,"mtu":1200,"ltr":true}}`
  (no new cap, as the audio rules require); the on-demand `camera_*` messages are C2/C4's.
- **Lip sync**: the PC knows, from `kSr`, the Mac capture time of every audio sample and video frame it
  plays. It then delays whichever path is faster (today audio Mac mic -> PC app ~108 ms, video target
  below) by the difference, video by holding frames, audio through the jitter buffer target.

Engine work list (Phase 2 WP sketch): `VideoSendStream` (AU in from the encoder with capture time,
fragment, seal, history, bucket, NACK/PLI handling -> encoder callbacks `force_ltr_refresh(token)` /
`force_idr()`, `set_bitrate()`), `VideoRecvStream` (reassembly by frame, hole detection + NACK, frame
deadline, decode order, `kPli`, RR fields, hand-off of complete AUs to the decoder/virtual camera with
their capture time), `kSr` for every stream, replay window size per stream, codec id 2, the RR payload
above. Tests: the sim's policy cases as unit tests (hole -> NACK -> repair; stall -> PLI; LTR ack flow).

## Q3: recovery policy (replay of measured traces, D = 80 ms)

720p, `v720b` trace (burst sending, 2.5 Mbit/s), keyframes at C1's 120 KB. "frozen" = display ticks
without a new frame; "degraded" = frames shown with concealment artefacts (intra refresh only).
Full tables, with the 1.5 s stall/hold overlays and the 1080p trace: `proto/transport/out/q3_*.md`.

| policy | measured trace: frozen ms/min (freezes/min) | + 0.5 % bursty loss: frozen / degraded ms/min | + 1 % iid loss: frozen / degraded | + 1.5 s stall / 5 min: longest freeze | kbit/s (at 1 % loss) | added delay |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| IDR every 2 s, no feedback | 221 (0.5) | 10,029 / 0 | 44,497 / 0 | 2,500 ms | 2,617 | none; freezes until the next IDR |
| IDR on PLI | 47 (0.6) | 1,350 / 0 | 11,610 / 0 | 1,600 ms | 4,136 | a loss = 3-12 frames frozen; 120-256 KB bursts |
| LTR refresh on PLI | 47 (0.6) | 1,350 / 0 | 11,362 / 0 | 1,600 ms | 2,675 | same freezes, no big burst |
| intra refresh 1 s (+PLI) | 33 (0.6) | 575 / 10,219 (2,480) | 3,883 / 48,003 (17,261) | 1,500 ms | 2,617 (3,738) | smooth bitrate; up to 1 s of smears per loss |
| FEC 1/10 + PLI | 37 (0.5) | 1,076 / 0 | 2,192 / 0 | 1,600 ms | 3,545 (+15 %) | parity after each group; useless on bursts |
| **NACK + LTR refresh** | **33 (0.6)** | **177 / 0** | **84 / 0** | **1,433 ms** | **2,644** | a repaired frame arrives ~1 RTT + detection later (5-35 ms), within D |
| NACK + IDR on PLI | 33 (0.6) | 177 / 0 | 84 / 0 | 1,733 ms | 2,644 | same, IDR after a stall |
| NACK + LTR + FEC 1/10 | 30 (0.5) | 174 / 0 | 54 / 0 | 1,433 ms | 3,011 | +15 % rate for 3-30 ms/min less |

Reading: on this Wi-Fi the RTT is 3-8 ms (p99 ~35 ms), so a retransmission almost always lands before a
70-80 ms deadline. NACK is what removes freezes, and it costs only the lost packets. When repair is
impossible (a stall, a hole > 64 packets, a retransmission lost 3 times), an **LTR refresh** restarts
decoding with a P-frame-sized frame (C1: 12-14 KB vs 120/256 KB for an IDR): no extra burst, no bitrate
spike, the same freeze length as an IDR. FEC buys nothing here: losses come in runs (1-3, or 28-82
packets), and it costs 15-30 % of the rate. Intra refresh never freezes long but smears for up to a
second after every loss, and it needs `PeriodicRefreshMode`, which C1 is testing. **Recommendation:
NACK (<= 3 tries, within the frame deadline, holes <= 64 packets) + LTR refresh on PLI, IDR only at
stream start or when no acknowledged LTR is left (or after 3 unanswered refreshes). No FEC, no
periodic intra refresh, no periodic IDR.** The 1080p trace gives the same ranking (`q3_v1080b.md`).

| 60 ms | 87 (1.9) | 97 (1.7) | 110 (2.3) | 7 (0.2) | 10 (0.3) | 64 (1.3) / 54 (1.1) |
| **70 ms** | **47 (1.0)** | **60 (1.0)** | **64 (1.0)** | 3 (0.1) | 7 (0.2) | 40 (0.9) / 43 (0.8) |
| 80 ms | 33 (0.6) | 43 (0.7) | 40 (0.5) | 3 (0.1) | 3 (0.1) | 30 (0.6) / 33 (0.5) |
| 100 ms | 17 (0.4) | 23 (0.5) | 30 (0.4) | 0 | 0 | 17 (0.4) / 27 (0.4) |

- **Target: D ~ 70 ms** for <= 1 frozen/late frame per minute on today's noisiest traces; 40 ms would
  do in calm periods. Implement it adaptively like the audio buffer: target = p99 of the last 10 s of
  frame completion delays (network part + pacing) + 8 ms, rises at once, falls slowly (<= 15 ms per
  0.5 s), floor 50 ms, cap 150 ms. On the measured traces it averaged within 2 ms of the floor,
  because the p99 was below it. The end-to-end budget then reads: capture + encode (C1: ~5 ms encode
  at 720p, plus the camera's own pipeline) + 50-70 ms + decode (C2) + virtual camera hand-off (C2). That
  is inside the 100-150 ms target if C1+C2 stay under ~50 ms together.
- **Late frames**: decode them (they are references) but never show them late: the display shows the
  newest frame whose deadline has not passed. No latency debt accumulates.
- **Stall of 1.5 s**: if the AP holds the packets and releases them (`hold` overlay), every frame
  arrives, the decoder runs through the backlog (45 frames; at C1's 1.3-3.8 ms decode per 720p frame,
  60-170 ms), and the display jumps to the newest frame. Freeze = stall length (1,433 ms for 1.5 s), no
  keyframe needed. If packets were lost (`stall` overlay), NACK gives up (hole > 64 packets) and the PLI
  -> LTR refresh restarts decoding one RTT after the stall: same freeze (1,433 ms). With an IDR instead:
  +300 ms. The receiver should also drop the queued backlog older than D + 150 ms without displaying
  it, and hold the last frame (not black) during the stall.
- **Adaptive bitrate: not needed for congestion on this LAN.** 5 Mbit/s showed the same loss and delay
  tail as 2.5 Mbit/s or audio alone: no queue build-up with load, the delay spikes are radio events. Keep
  one **safety step-down** driven by the RR: if, over 2 s, post-repair loss > 2 % or the playout margin
  is < 0 for > 10 % of frames, step the encoder down (5 -> 2.5 -> 1.2 Mbit/s, `AverageBitRate` can
  change live, C1 `t_br`); step up after 10 s clean. Also apply it on the TCP path (Q5).

## Q5: TCP fallback over the Beamer link

Measured (stand-in: one TCP connection through `ssh -L` over the same Wi-Fi, audio-like and video on the
same stream): without loss, TCP is as fast as UDP. Frame p99 29-32 ms; audio p95 6.3-7.4 ms, against
6.8 ms over UDP. The one Wi-Fi event in those 15 min (a 0.5 s stall, UDP RTT 497 ms) became **652 ms
of delay for audio and video together**, with ~12 frames > 300 ms late. That is head-of-line blocking:
everything behind the stalled bytes waits, and on the real link that includes input events and their
acks. TCP also turns every loss into a stall of >= 1 RTT (fast retransmit) or an RTO (>= 200 ms). Not
seen here because nothing was lost; UDP loss was 0-0.05 %.

The production fallback is worse than this stand-in. Media go as base64 JSON `audio_frame` messages on
the Python link (+33 %, ~4 packets per message). At 2.5 Mbit/s that is ~65 messages/s through the link
threads, and the outbox limit of 64 is shared with input (PHASE2 §4.5).

Recommendation for the `link` state:
- **Video continues, but small**: switch the encoder to ~1 Mbit/s (720p at 15 fps, or 640x360 at 30) by
  the same `set_bitrate` path. Measured over TCP at 1 Mbit/s: no event > 43 ms.
- **Video only on the link**, no UDP duplicate (audio's "send on both" is there for instant recovery
  detection; the hellos already do that for the flow).
- **NACK off, PLI/LTR on**: TCP repairs; a frame Beamer drops is a hole, answered by an LTR refresh.
- **Video is dropped first**: drop video `link_out` packets when the outbox holds > 32 messages (audio's
  limit stays 64). Drop whole frames from the oldest, then request an LTR refresh.
- **Pause** (hold the last frame on the PC) if the outbox stayed > 32 for 1 s. Resume with an IDR when
  UDP is back (the flow returns to `up` after 1 s of UDP, PHASE2 §4.5).
- If the link is the SSH tunnel fallback (127.0.0.1), pause video altogether.

## Risks

- **One run per configuration, weather varies**: `v720b` had 3.6 episodes > 45 ms/min, `v1080h` 0.2.
  Burst-vs-bucket was not run back to back; the 1080p burst loss mechanism is consistent (3 keyframes,
  the same fragment threshold), but its frequency is a 10-minute sample.
- **No real 1.5 s stall today**: stall behaviour comes from overlays, not from a measured stall.
- **Python on the PC** timestamps arrivals after a GIL hand-off: adds up to ~1-2 ms of noise to the
  tail, not to the medians. The C++ engine will be slightly better, not worse.
- **TCP stand-in** = ssh over Tailscale, not Beamer's link. Its JSON/b64 path and Python threads will
  add delay and CPU (to measure in C4/Phase 2).
- **Keyframe-triggered holes**: 4 events in 70 min of UDP video. The mechanism (driver or AP queue
  during contention) is inferred, not observed. IDR pacing at 4x the bitrate was not run as such (2.5x
  and 25 Mbit/s were): a Phase 2 soak should log any hole > 5 packets together with the frame type.
- **The sim's channel does not depend on load**: the burst-loss mechanism above is not modelled, which
  favours IDR-based policies.
- **LAN only**: the Mac and the PC on the same AP. Another topology (wired PC, 2.4 GHz, a busy
  neighbourhood) would change the numbers, not the design.

## Recommendation for Phase 2 (summary)

1. Video is a stream kind `video` on the existing audio flow, in the same helper, codec id 2, packet v1
   header unchanged; 12 B video header in the payload; 1148 B fragments; capture time in `ts`.
2. Add `kNack` (7), `kPli` (8), `kSr` (9); the 24 B video RR; a 1024-packet replay window for video;
   retransmit the identical sealed packet from a 1 s history.
3. Video pacer: P/LTR frames through a 32-packet / 25 Mbit/s bucket (in effect unpaced), IDRs paced at
   2.5-4x the bitrate; audio/control bypass it.
4. Recovery: NACK (<= 3 tries, holes <= 64 packets, within the deadline) + LTR refresh on PLI; IDR at
   start or as the last resort. No FEC, no periodic IDR, no intra refresh.
5. Receiver: adaptive playout delay (p99 + 8 ms, 50-150 ms, ~70 ms here); decode late frames, show only
   on time; jump to the newest after a stall; drop backlog older than D + 150 ms.
6. Bitrate: fixed (2.5 / 5 Mbit/s) + safety step-down from RR loss/margin; in `link` state 1 Mbit/s,
   video only on the link, dropped before audio, paused after 1 s of backlog.
7. Lip sync from `kSr` on both streams; delay the faster path on the PC.
